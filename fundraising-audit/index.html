The five things LPs test before they commit

Twenty multiple-choice questions, four in each area. Each area gets its own score, so you can see exactly where to start.

Track record and edge
LPs underwrite people. If they can't see exactly what you did and why it will repeat, they can't back it.
LP pipeline
Most raises stall on pipeline, not on the pitch. You need far more qualified conversations than feels reasonable.
Materials and diligence
Diligence speed signals how you'll run the fund. A slow, incomplete data room reads as a slow, incomplete firm.
Terms and economics
Terms and fee runway tell an LP whether you'll still be standing in year three, and whether your interests line up with theirs.
Process and momentum
Momentum closes funds. Without a date and a minimum, every LP has a reason to wait for the next one to commit.

How the score works

Every answer is worth zero to three points. Your total is shown out of 100, with a red flag for any answer that would stop a conversation on its own.

  • 80 to 100: Ready to raise
  • 60 to 79: Nearly ready
  • 40 to 59: Not yet
  • Below 40: Rebuild the plan

Your answers stay in your browser. We don't store them.

Who it's for

Venture fund managers preparing to raise, especially emerging managers on Fund I or II. It's most useful three to six months before your first LP meetings, when there's still time to fix what it finds. Ask each partner to take it separately, then compare scores.
