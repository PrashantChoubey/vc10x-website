How it works

Check in weeklyYour positioning against your own targets in ten asset classes, your risk appetite, and what's driving your decisions. Last week's answers are prefilled, so you only change what moved.
See your peersSubmit and the results open: where CIOs are over- and underweight, which way they plan to move, and how risk appetite is shifting week to week.
React to eventsAfter a market-moving event, such as a central bank decision or a geopolitical shock, a short extra round shows how peers responded within days.

What you'll see

  • Net positioning in each asset class: the share of CIOs overweight minus the share underweight, against their own targets.
  • Where they plan to add or reduce over the next three months.
  • Risk appetite this week, how it has moved over the past month, and the trend over time.
  • The macro drivers CIOs say matter most right now, and how private-market commitments are pacing against plan.
  • Splits by organization type and size, and your own answers next to the group's.

Your answers stay private

  • Results only appear for groups of 8 or more CIOs. A smaller group or segment stays hidden.
  • No individual answers, names or organizations are ever shown to other members or published.
  • Your answers are stored against your private link so you can update them during the week. Only VC10X can see who has checked in, never alongside what they answered.
  • Optional comments may be quoted in the weekly summary without your name, organization or anything that identifies you.

Questions

Why only 8 or more?

Below that, a determined reader could start to guess who answered what. Every figure, including each segment and each asset class, needs at least 8 answers before it appears.

What does "overweight" mean here?

Against your own strategic or policy target, not against a benchmark or other members. A CIO at their target is neutral, whatever that target is.

Why weekly, not daily?

CIOs don't rebalance daily, and a daily survey wouldn't get answered. A weekly round with extra rounds after big events catches real shifts without wasting your time.

Is this investment advice?

No. The Pulse reports what participating CIOs say about their own portfolios. It isn't a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything.
